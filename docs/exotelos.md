# Exotelos

Binding law for this repo. The README is the short form. This page is the drawing.

## Definition

An origin's interest, in tertiary, in exogenous intention of perpendicular interest on a separate grid.

In future time the other origin must also develop their independent exotelos, separate from all current origins.

Interest may change, but exotelos only exists in tertiary interest that does not affect the primary or secondary interest.

Exotelos may fade in time.

## Example

The artist's focus on oil painting (primary interest) and charcoal sketching (secondary interest) was entirely separate from her exotelos, which was a detached hope that a musician she admired would eventually explore the intention of merging classical composition with electronic beats.

## Endotelos

New points on the same grid.

## Drawing a single axis

Opposites are needed to describe the directions of the axis.

## Drawing a perpendicular axis

Opposites are needed to describe the directions of independence from the other axis.

## Drawing the origin

The origin is at the center of four opposites that are perpendicular but independent.

- Opposites create alignments. As you move from one you align more with the other. That move is a rotation from the extremes, along the axis.
- Adjusting an alignment is that rotation. A rotation needs a pivot.
- The pivot is the origin: the center of four opposites that are perpendicular but independent.
- Rotating from one opposite to the other changes your alignment on the perpendicular by a proportional amount defined by the rotation.
- Alignment on the perpendicular axis creates a primary and secondary axis based on that alignment.

## Drawing the exotelos

1. Define an origin.
2. Within that origin find two interests that are independent from each other.
3. On a separate grid define an origin and two more interests that are independent.
4. Define the first origin's interest in the intention for the other origin's interest.
5. That interest is tertiary to the first origin.
6. Tertiary matters because it does not affect primary and secondary, so it can stay external.
7. Draw the exotelos for the other origin's interest.

Drawing the other origin's exotelos is recursive.

Each recursion is an increase in time.

As time progresses, alignments may move but they are still in the frame of an exotelos.

The frame of an exotelos does not define how time flows. It only exists inside its constraints of time.

An exotelos may fade with time.

## Deterministic expansion of axis

Axis stem out into smaller subsets.

Example: 1 origin, 2 core, 4 metaphysical, 8 physical.

- From an origin, make two core axes.
- Each core axis makes two metaphysical axes.
- Each metaphysical axis makes two physical axes.

## Deterministic compression of axis

Axis combine into larger supersets.

Example: 8 physical, 4 metaphysical, 2 core, 1 origin.

- Start with 8 physical axes.
- Each pair of physical axes makes one metaphysical axis.
- Each pair of metaphysical axes makes one core axis.
- Those core axes form an origin.

An axis can collapse if the exotelos fades, or for any other reason it collapses.

Non-deterministic expansion / compression is where an axis collapses, so some portion of the expansion / compression changes.

Even if the axis collapses, the unaffected tertiary still exists: the exotelos.

## Temporal expansion of exotelos

Time has passed and the child axis collapsed except for the exotelos.

New child axes are made from the origin inside the exotelos by expansion of axis.

## Temporal compression of exotelos

Time has passed and the parent axis collapsed except for the exotelos.

New parent axes are made from the origin inside the exotelos by compression of axis.

Things within any axis all have an origin, two axes, and a perception of exotelos.

## Drawing deterministic expansion

1. Define an origin.
2. Define an interest that origin has.
3. Define an independent interest that origin has.
4. Define an exotelos.
5. Do deterministic axis expansion on both interests.

## Drawing deterministic compression

1. Define the axis.
2. Do deterministic axis compression until two interests are made.
3. Define an origin.
4. Define an exotelos.

## Drawing out actions

Actions take an origin in the direction of an interest. Actions happen over an amount of time.

Pick an origin, then find what is wanted to align the origin more to the interest. Consider what will happen to the perpendicular interest when you align, and how much time it would take. Time is proportional to the amount of rotation for alignment.

There is a direct relationship between strong alignment and the time a large rotation would take. Stronger alignment makes a smaller rotation easier.

When an interest fades in the middle of an action, alignment goes to the other interest entirely.

## Persistence and unknown

Everyone has a life outside of writing, where they go out and do stuff. They have their own primary and secondary interests. When writing, imagine your exotelos to be writing whatever it is that you are writing.

You write something that will persist past even you and your primary and secondary interest.

Any unknown lasts beyond the axis currently known.

For un-determinism, unknown exists.

True randomness or chaos only brings more discovery and unknowns. Exotelos has infinite spaces; chaos / true randomness brings more depth to the unfamiliar and potential for higher-order persistence after collapse of the axis.

Unknown is a catalyst for exotelos.
